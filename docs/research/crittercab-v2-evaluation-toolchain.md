## The decision in two sentences

Erik is deciding whether CritterCab should become a deliberate "v2" in the CritterCommerce to CritterMart sense (a new version that acts on lessons, folds bounded contexts, and drops what did not earn its keep), and if so whether that happens as a new repository, in place behind a declared boundary, as a remaster of conventions with no boundary, or not at all. The non-negotiable backdrop is the portfolio's 2026-10-05 Event Modeling decision: a committed curated `*.emodel.yaml` is the authored surface in every Critter repo, the derived rung from the running app is always on and never authored, Gherkin via Bobcat is the executable layer, and markdown workshops are minutes.

I evaluated from the build outward: what runs, what is pinned but unused, what the first declared file would have to say, what the first Bobcat binding would need, and what the three-transport, two-store, service-per-BC topology costs to keep honest in a file-based Aspire apphost and a Testcontainers CI. Every claim below was spot-checked against `$HOME/mnt/CritterCab` at HEAD `4d8bde4`.

## Numbered evaluation points

### 1. The code is a seed, not a system, so "v2 of the code" is not the question

`src/CritterCab.Dispatch` is 28 source files (1,441 lines including `Program.cs`), `src/CritterCab.Telemetry` is 15 files (901 lines), and `tests/` holds 54 `[Fact]`/`[Theory]` methods across four collections (`Dispatch` x4 classes, `DispatchKafka` x2, `Telemetry` x4, `TelemetryKafka` x1). That is roughly 2,300 lines of production code implementing eight of 44 modeled slices. There is nothing here to "rewrite" in the CritterMart sense; the Dispatch aggregate, the four inline projections, the Telemetry gRPC ingest and the Kafka publish/consume pair are all code the retros describe as having paid off, and all of it was written under conventions worth keeping (feature folders, seams one PR early, connection-string guards so a broker-less `dotnet run` still boots, both verified in `src/CritterCab.Dispatch/Program.cs` and `src/CritterCab.Telemetry/Program.cs`).

What is large and does need versioning is the declared surface around the code: 19 Accepted ADRs, a v0.8 vision with eleven BCs, a `Directory.Packages.props` with seven pins nothing references, 44 skill directories, and routing files (README, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md) that describe a repo that no longer exists. "v2" is meaningful for CritterCab only as a reset of what the repository claims about itself. The design corpus is the asset; the code survives unchanged; the claims get a boundary.

### 2. The pins and the apphost tell the honest story of what runs

`Directory.Packages.props` pins WolverineFx 6.21.0 across eleven packages. A `grep` of every `.csproj` under `src/` and `tests/` for `Polecat`, `AzureServiceBus`, `SignalR`, `MsSql`, `ServiceBus` and `SqlServer` returns nothing: `WolverineFx.Polecat`, `WolverineFx.AzureServiceBus`, `WolverineFx.SignalR`, `Aspire.Hosting.SqlServer`, `Microsoft.AspNetCore.SignalR.Client`, `Testcontainers.MsSql` and `Testcontainers.ServiceBus` are declared and never referenced. The props file even annotates the SignalR client as "integration-test transport for Relay BC push assertions" for a Relay BC that appears in no workshop.

`apphost.cs` is self-consistent and honest: one Postgres with two databases, one Kafka broker, two projects on a fixed port band, `#:property ManagePackageVersionsCentrally=false` to escape CPM. But its own header comment admits the Aspire versions in `Directory.Packages.props` "are NOT consumed by the current file-based apphost.cs" and must be kept in lockstep by hand. `.github/workflows/dotnet.yml` restores, builds and tests `CritterCab.slnx`, and its solution-completeness guard walks `find . -name '*.csproj'`, which by construction never sees a file-based app. `docs/skills/DEBT.md` records that this broke for two weeks unnoticed and that a second compile error was caught only locally. Any session editing the AppHost is still its own CI.

Two more build artifacts are declared and unexercised: `protos/buf.yaml` configures `buf lint` with a scoped `ignore_only` for the Telemetry RPC names, but no CI step runs buf and the retros say it was never installed locally; `protos/buf.gen.yaml` emits Go output (`buf.build/protocolbuffers/go`, `buf.build/grpc/go`) that no project consumes. The proto tree holds seven files, and the two csproj files compile exactly two of them (`report_locations.proto` with `GrpcServices="Both"` in Telemetry, `driver_location_updated.proto` with `GrpcServices="None"` in both services, deliberately not a shared assembly, with the `Type.FullName` identity reasoning in the Dispatch csproj comment). The dispatch and pricing protos are authored contracts with no compiler behind them.

The pattern across pins, apphost, buf and protos is the same one the 2026-06-25 state-of-the-repo note named: declared ahead of exercised. That is the v1 failure mode the toolchain has to design out, and it is the same thing the portfolio decision forbids in a different register ("no asserted status in any file").

### 3. Two of three transports are real, and the third is the most expensive one to keep honest

gRPC and Kafka are built, wired and under test. `src/CritterCab.Telemetry/Program.cs` maps `AddWolverineGrpc()` and `MapWolverineGrpcServices()`, and the Kafka publish is configured with `SendInline()`, `UseIdempotentProducer()`, endpoint-scoped `UseProtobufSerialization()` and a process-global `opts.Durability.UseSyncRetryBlock = true` with a comment that correctly flags it as a producer concern to re-weigh when a second publisher arrives. `src/CritterCab.Dispatch/Program.cs` listens with `ConfigureConsumer(c => c.GroupId = "dispatch")`, `BeginAtLatest()`, `UseProtobufSerialization()` and `DefaultIncomingMessage<DriverLocationUpdated>()`. Every row of the Kafka column in `docs/decisions/019-transport-agnostic-topic-naming.md` is exercised in code under test; the ADR says so and the code agrees.

Azure Service Bus has no code, no emulator in `apphost.cs` (port 5393 is reserved in a comment only), and no fixture. Keeping it honest means adding the ASB emulator (which itself needs SQL edge) to the apphost, `Testcontainers.ServiceBus` fixtures, and at least two more images to the serial pre-pull list in `dotnet.yml`, a list that exists only because six containers across two parallel test assemblies already saturated the runner's Docker Hub connection. ASB is the transport whose carrying cost in CI is highest and whose showcase value, from the toolchain seat, is moderate: Wolverine's ASB transport is well documented upstream, and CritterCab's distinctive capability list in the portfolio map (gRPC, Kafka, service-per-BC, polyglot, identity ACL, parked Polecat) does not include it.

Against that, `docs/decisions/018-candidate-projection-ownership-and-telemetry-geospatial-supply.md` is the best teaching artifact in the repo precisely because `NearbyAvailableDrivers` is a two-transport join (Kafka location, ASB availability) folded at the consumer's invariant, and ADR-019's per-transport semantics table (sessions plus outbox on ASB, partition key plus no outbox plus `serverReceivedAt` dedup on Kafka) is the document a future BC consults. Drop ASB and both ADRs become fiction. My position: ASB stays, but it stops being "committed" in the ADR-005 sense until the Driver Profile availability slice builds it, and v2 accepts the emulator and the pre-pull cost as the price of ADR-018 being true. The alternative (route availability over Kafka too) would be cheaper in CI and would quietly demolish the argument ADR-018 was written to make.

### 4. What the first declared `*.emodel.yaml` would need to say, and why it is per service

Under the portfolio decision the derived rung comes from the running app. In a service-per-BC topology that means one derived rung per Wolverine host: Dispatch's would surface the `POST /api/rides/request` endpoint, the `RideRequest` stream and its registered event types, `FareQuoteAutomation` and `CandidateSelectionAutomation` (discovered via `CustomizeHandlerDiscovery(d => d.Includes.WithNameSuffix("Automation"))`), the four inline projections, the Kafka listener on `telemetry.driver-location-updated`, `DriverLocationUpdatedHandler`, and the `DriverAvailabilityChanged` handler with no transport bound. Telemetry's would surface the `ReportLocations` gRPC service, the `TelemetryPolicy` stream, the Kafka publication, and `EvictStalePositions`.

What the derived rung cannot show, and what the declared file therefore must carry, is the meaning: that `AvailableDriver` is a plain LWW document and not a projection (the long comment in Dispatch's `AddMarten` block explains why), that the throttle is cell-change-or-heartbeat, that the H3 k-ring query is the candidate source, that `PricingClientStub` and `DispatchPolicySnapshot.Default` are stubs for BCs not yet built, that the ASB half of ADR-018 is a forward constraint on Driver Profile, and that selection optimises recall while acceptance enforces correctness. None of that is in a descriptor; all of it is in W001, W006 and ADR-018.

Rungs merge by slice name. Today nothing in the repo has a stable slice name. W001 numbers slices 5.1 to 5.12, W006 numbers them 1 to 5, and W006 §6.5 is a Dispatch slice living in the Telemetry workshop (verified: `### 6.5 Slice 5 — W001 §5.3 amendment: Dispatch's NearbyAvailableDrivers population`). Test classes are `Slice1TelemetryPolicyTests`, `Slice53CandidatesSelectedTests` and so on. The first declared file for each service forces the one decision the `testing-fundamentals` DEBT row has been waiting on: slices get names, not numbers, and the test class names follow the slice names so `[BobcatSlice]` has something to bind to. That resolves a parked "decision, not cleanup" row by portfolio rule rather than taste, which is the cheapest way such rows ever get resolved.

The file should be per service (`src/CritterCab.Dispatch/dispatch.emodel.yaml`, `src/CritterCab.Telemetry/telemetry.emodel.yaml`, or wherever the convention lands), because the derived rung is per host and because `docs/rules/structural-constraints.md` forbids cross-service project references; a repo-level file would be a shared artifact no service owns. The cross-service slice (gRPC ingest to Kafka to document upsert) appears in both files as a Translation slice referencing the other service's slice by name, and `docs/context-map/README.md` remains the artifact that says which edges exist. The vocabulary inside the file is JasperFx's descriptor vocabulary; the vocabulary inside the workshops is Dymitruk's Command / View / Automation / Translation with Klefter and Bruun adjuncts; the mapping between them is one table, and it belongs in a corrected `docs/skills/event-modeling/SKILL.md` (which today misnames both adjunct authors and never names the information-completeness check). W004's "continue handler" and "start handler" are Wolverine process-manager words and must not leak into either the declared file or the pattern slot.

### 5. What the first Bobcat-bound slice would need, and why it is a realized slice

`[BobcatSlice]` binding on an existing test is metadata that points at a slice name in the declared file; it does not change what the test does. CritterCab's tests are Alba-hosted integration tests over Testcontainers (`postgres:18-alpine`, `confluentinc/cp-kafka:7.6.1`), so binding costs nothing at runtime. Scaffolding a new slice through Bobcat is different: it wants a GWT in the model and produces a Gherkin feature plus step bindings, and the repo already has 45 GWT scenarios in W001 and 26 in W002 written in markdown, so there is a translation step rather than an authoring step.

The first binding should be W001 slice 5.1 (`RideRequested`): HTTP plus Marten only, no Kafka container, the simplest green test in the repo. The second should be the cross-service slice, because binding it proves that a slice spanning two hosts and two test assemblies can be one named slice in two declared files. New-slice scaffolding should wait for Driver Profile's first slice, which is the first slice v2 writes from scratch.

Bobcat's initial release was planned for October 2026 and is not confirmed shipped as of today. The declared file does not depend on Bobcat; the binding does. v2 must therefore sequence declared file first, binding second, and must not block the Driver Profile workshop on Bobcat's availability. The repo's own retro 008 rule applies: run source verification at prompt authoring, log the HEAD of each verified upstream, and treat Bobcat's API as unverified until a session has read it.

### 6. CritterWatch is the thing that makes service-per-BC visible, and its backplane is a drift

CritterWatch 1.1 was expected around 2026-10-01 with an Event Store Explorer across Marten, Polecat and Fisher and event-model visualisation from the running app. In a one-host modular monolith the derived rung is one picture; in CritterCab's topology the two-service flow is two pictures unless something aggregates them, and CritterWatch is that something. The "derived rung always on" constraint therefore has a concrete cost per service: wire the descriptors, point every host at the backplane, and run the backplane in the apphost.

`docs/decisions/017-rabbitmq-for-critterwatch.md` makes RabbitMQ the tooling-only backplane; `docs/rules/structural-constraints.md` line 43 still says "**Do not use RabbitMQ.** RabbitMQ is explicitly excluded" with no carve-out. The trial licence expired 2026-07-10 per the handoff. CritterWatch is in the handoff's top three and in the portfolio's expectation for every repo, so v2 must settle the carve-out in the rules file, settle the licence, and add RabbitMQ to the apphost and (probably) a third pre-pull image to CI. It is a known, bounded cost; the risk is that it lands before the two-feeder problem in point 8 is solved, in which case CritterWatch shows a flow that always ends in `NoCandidatesAvailable`.

### 7. The ADR set needs a boundary, not a rewrite

All 19 ADRs under `docs/decisions/` are Accepted and several are no longer true of the repo or the stack. ADR-007 says conference demos include a reachable URL; the portfolio says nothing needs to be reachable. ADR-010 says "Polecat does not provide event sourcing"; the repo's own `docs/skills/polecat-event-sourcing/SKILL.md` describes Polecat 3.1 as an event store with DCB, async daemon and snapshots, so the repo already contradicts itself, and upstream Polecat is 5.x. ADR-005 says ASB is a committed transport; no code. ADR-015 and ADR-016 commit to SignalR, three SPAs and a Trips inline projection; no code. ADR-017 commits to a broker the rules file forbids. ADR-003's own supersession trigger ("a Critter-Stack-aware code-generation platform with permissive licensing") is arguably fired by Bobcat and the declared rung, depending on licences.

ADRs are not written in this session, and in any case rewriting 19 ADRs is the wrong move: they are the record. What v2 needs is a visible line that says "ADRs 001 to 019 are the v1 record; each is re-affirmed, amended or superseded by the first v2 PR that touches its subject." That is a tag plus a vision 1.0 plus a one-paragraph preface in `docs/decisions/README.md`, and it is exactly what an in-place boundary gives and a no-boundary remaster does not.

### 8. The two-service flow is a real showcase up to the last hop, and a demo with no second feeder after it

The flow is real: a client stream into Telemetry, throttle, protobuf on Kafka, document upsert in Dispatch, k-ring query. The handoff (`docs/planning/2026-07-25-post-pr47-w006-complete-design-return-due.md`) states the consequence plainly: "a real end-to-end run correctly yields `NoCandidatesAvailable`," because the availability side of `AvailableDriver` has never been fed. `src/CritterCab.Dispatch/AvailableDrivers/DriverAvailabilityChanged.cs` is a placeholder message with no transport bound, deliberately mirroring W006 §6.5's four availability fields so it "describes the shape of the hole." The availability-before-location drop is pinned by a test and does not self-heal.

So the first slice of v2 is not a choice; the repo already made it. It is the Driver Profile availability slice: `DriverCameOnline` (and its siblings) published from a Driver Profile service, consumed by Dispatch, folding into the availability side of the document the Kafka listener already maintains. That is the slice that turns the cross-service flow from a demo into a showcase, the slice that builds the third transport, and the slice whose workshop the handoff already ranks first. It is also the slice where Polecat enters, which brings me to placement.

### 9. Polecat placement: (a) Driver Profile, in v2's first arc, sequenced after the workshop

Service-per-BC removes CritterBids' failure mode (two stores claiming the main store in one host) by construction; each host has one store. Dispatch and Telemetry stay on Marten (they are built, tested and Postgres-shaped). The question is which of the next BCs runs Polecat.

Option (b), Payments-first per the vision and per the `polecat-event-sourcing` skill's framing, has the exact defect ADR-018 rejected in its Option A: a producer with no consumer. Payments has no workshop, no feeder (Trips does not exist, fare settlement has nothing to settle), and would be a Polecat store with a seeded stream and no second service in the conversation. Option (a), Driver Profile on Polecat, has a consumer on day one (Dispatch's placeholder handler), a modeled consumer contract (W006 §6.5, ADR-018), a per-driver aggregate with a real invariant (availability transitions), and a Dispatch test that already pins the drop case. It also makes Polecat's event store the thing on display, which is precisely the claim ADR-010 got wrong and needs amending in v2.

The toolchain cost is real and already half-paid: `Aspire.Hosting.SqlServer` and `Testcontainers.MsSql` are pinned, the apphost comment reserves port 5391 for SQL Server, and the pre-pull list grows by one large image. The risk is API churn: the skill is written for Polecat 3.0/3.1 schema and the CLAUDE.md table says "Polecat 3.1+"; upstream is 5.x, so the skill is unverified and must be treated as such. Sequencing matters: the Driver Profile workshop needs no store decision; the Driver Profile skeleton PR is where Polecat lands; the availability slice PR is where ASB lands. Three new infrastructure pieces (SQL Server, Polecat, ASB emulator) in one arc is a lot, and v1's retros say a seam one PR early paid off twice while bundling hit its fourth confirmation unencoded. Two PRs of groundwork (store, then transport) before the slice is the shape that worked for Telemetry.

### 10. BC disposition and service count

ADR-002 says six to eight services from eleven BCs and promised ADR-010 would finalise the topology; it never did. From the build outward, every service costs a database, an apphost block, a test project with its own fixture, a pre-pull entry, an xUnit collection and a derived rung, and each new fixture has already nearly broken CI once. Service count is the dominant carrying cost, so v2 should declare a ceiling and a build order rather than an inventory.

### 12. The skill library is the heaviest thing in the repo and the least load-bearing

44 entries under `docs/skills/` (41 skills plus `DEBT.md`, `README.md`, `_template`), about 18,300 lines. Fourteen describe technology with no code behind them (`polecat-document-store`, `polecat-event-sourcing`, `wolverine-azure-service-bus`, `wolverine-sagas`, `distributed-saga-considerations`, `dynamic-consistency-boundary`, `identity-acl`, `marten-async-daemon`, `observability-metrics`, `observability-tracing`, `polyglot-go-service`, `aspire-service-defaults`, `cli-azure-messaging`, most of `testing-advanced`). Only four skill names appear in code comments. `DEBT.md` has 18 open rows, three of them decisions, and its own history records that skills authored ahead of code contradicted the APIs from day one (the `wolverine-grpc-bidirectional-handlers` row is a whole skill premised on a limitation 6.21 removed). Nine directory names are duplicated in CritterBids and the library layers over JasperFx's paid ai-skills.

The v2 rule that follows from point 2 is that a skill, like a pin, exists in CritterCab only when the repo has code that exercises it. The fourteen no-code skills are archived (an `archive/` path under `docs/skills/` is the one place a `v1/`-style move is safe, because the retros cite them by name and nothing in code links to them). The grounded skills stay: `wolverine-kafka`, `wolverine-grpc-handlers`, `wolverine-marten-automation`, `marten-projections`, `marten-wolverine-aggregates`, `service-bootstrap`, `testing-integration`, `aspire`, `protobuf-contracts`, `vertical-slice-organization`, `csharp-coding-standards`, `domain-event-conventions`, and a corrected `event-modeling`. Whether the methodology substrate wants one portfolio home is an owner question; from the toolchain seat the answer is that whatever is shared should be vendored with a lockfile the way the Pocock skills under `.agents/skills/` already are, not copied.

### 13. Why not a new repository

A fresh repo gets a csproj apphost, correct pins, Bobcat from commit one and no 230-pattern settings file, and the surviving code is 2,300 lines that would move unchanged. But everything of value that is not code is cross-referenced by path and by PR number: 33 retros cite PR #42 through #47, the handoff cites `327916d`, workshops cite ADR candidates by section, and the repo recorded that deleting one planning note broke 14 cross-references in 7 files. A seeded repo either carries all of it (and then it is the same repo with a new URL and no history) or leaves it behind (and then the retros, which the methodology ranks above external docs as a source of truth, are in an archive nobody reads). The toolchain gain is achievable in place in one boundary PR. The 54 direct commits to `main` and the two stale tidy branches are not a reason to start over; they are a reason to add branch protection and merge or close `tidy/ci-bump-actions-v5` and `tidy/housekeeping-claude-md-status-refresh`.

### 14. Why not a remaster with no boundary, and why not continuing as is

Option 3 has the right content and the wrong shape. The cadence rules (`docs/prompts/README.md`: one prompt, one session, one PR, no opportunistic edits outside the prompt's files) are what kept v1 honest, and under those rules fixing the drift in README, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, both service READMEs, three workshop headers, the vision, the pins, the apphost, buf, the rules file and the ADR preface is a dozen tidy PRs. v1 recorded exactly that outcome: three housekeeping micro-PRs to encode two rules, and paperwork outweighing the decision for the narrative-skip session. A declared boundary is the legitimate exception the cadence already admits ("skeleton plus first slice; session-blocking skill fixes"), applied once, on purpose, with a tag so the before and after are both inspectable. Without a boundary, the ADRs that are no longer true stay Accepted with no marker and the next contributor cannot tell which commitments are live.

Option 4 would run the Driver Profile workshop under an `event-modeling` skill that misnames its sources and teaches "four building blocks" with Screens, in a repo whose authored surface is markdown that the portfolio has just decided is minutes. The workshop would produce minutes with no declared file to land in, and then a second session would have to transcribe them. Doing the workshop after the boundary costs one session of delay and saves the transcription.

### 15. Where v2 must not repeat v1, stated as toolchain rules

Nothing is pinned, configured, wired in the apphost, or described in a skill unless CI exercises it in the same PR or the next one. An ADR's status is a claim about code; an Accepted ADR with no exercising code gets a note saying so. The AppHost is a project in the solution. The pre-pull list is generated from the fixtures or checked by a test, not maintained by hand (the DEBT row says "nothing enforces it"). Routing files (README, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md) are short and point at the declared files and the running app, because the retros proved that append-only artifacts stay honest and routing files decay silently. Workshops stay minutes with a header that says so, and the derived rung plus the declared file are what say what is built, so no header ever again has to say "Complete" or "In progress."

What v2 keeps from v1 unchanged: GWT scenarios that translate directly to tests (now via Bobcat), seams one PR early, the spec-delta loop, verify-before-wiring at prompt authoring, the two-axis code review, logging the HEAD of each verified upstream, the connection-string guards, the solution-completeness guard, independent proto compilation per service with the `csharp_namespace` coupling documented, and the retro-per-PR habit.

## Recommendation

**Option 2, v2 in place behind a declared boundary, carrying Option 3's content.** The same repository, same history, same issue and PR numbers. The boundary is: a tag at `4d8bde4` marking the end of v1; vision 1.0 with the BC disposition from point 10 and the Azure tertiary goals and SPAs moved to "not in v2"; a preface in `docs/decisions/README.md` declaring ADRs 001 to 019 the v1 record, each re-affirmed, amended or superseded by the first v2 PR that touches its subject; the fourteen no-code skills moved to `docs/skills/archive/`; and the first two declared `*.emodel.yaml` files (Dispatch, Telemetry) curated from the eight realized slices, with slice names replacing slice numbers in both files and in the test class names. Workshops, retros, prompts and ADRs do not move; a `v1/` path for documents would break the cross-reference web for no gain.

The code is not versioned because there is nothing to version; v2 is a reset of what the repository claims and of the conventions under which it grows. That is what "v2" meant in the CritterMart lineage once you subtract the rewrite, and the rewrite is not needed here.

## Costs and risks

The boundary PR is large and violates the one-prompt-one-PR rule by design; it must be declared as the cadence exception in its prompt, with its own retro, and must touch no handler. Its blast radius is the apphost conversion (a new csproj in the slnx, CPM re-enabled, Aspire pins reconciled) and the Wolverine bump, both of which can break the Kafka and gRPC suites; the mitigation is to run them as two PRs if the bump surfaces anything.

Bobcat and CritterWatch 1.1 may not have shipped. The declared file and the slice renames do not depend on either; the `[BobcatSlice]` binding and the derived-rung display do. If Bobcat slips, v2 proceeds with Alba tests bound by naming convention only, which is still an improvement, and binds later. If CritterWatch's licence or backplane is unresolved, the derived rung is still "on" at the descriptor level inside each host; it is only the aggregated picture that waits.

Three infrastructure additions (SQL Server, Polecat, ASB emulator) arrive within one arc, and CI's Docker Hub saturation problem scales with them. The mitigation is sequencing (store PR, then transport PR, then slice PR) and making the pre-pull list enforced before the first new fixture lands. The Polecat skill is written against 3.x and upstream is 5.x; every Polecat API in it is unverified and must be sourced at prompt authoring.

The declared file could become the next "skill written ahead of code" if it is populated with all 44 modeled slices on day one. Curate the eight realized slices first; add Trips' twelve only when Trips enters the arc, and never transcribe W004 or W005 until their BCs do.

Velocity: v1 shipped eight implementation PRs in four months (18 percent of PRs). A six-service horizon at that rate is years. The disposition table is a ceiling and an order, not a promise, and the vision should say so.
